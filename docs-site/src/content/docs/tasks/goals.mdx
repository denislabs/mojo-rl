A task's `goal=` line is a small predicate expression:

```ini
goal=Near(brick, cube_a, 0.06)
goal=In(akita_black_bowl_1, wooden_cabinet_1_top_region)
goal=Joint(flat_stove_1_button, ge, 0.5)
```

The goal is the task's definition of success, and it is evaluated **where the
reward is computed** — inside the GPU reward kernel, per lane, every step. A
per-step round trip to the host at 1024 lanes would dominate the step; LIBERO
itself calls its success check from Python every step, which is the design
this avoids.

## Two passes: parse, then bind

`noeira/tasks/predicates.mojo` handles the language in two steps:

1. **`parse_goal`** turns the text into a tree of terms that still hold
   *names*.
2. **`bind_goal`** resolves every name against the family and the composed
   model, once, at task load. The result is a `BoundGoal`: a flat list of
   `(op, a, b, param)` terms holding body, site, region and joint indices.

A kernel never sees a string. Terms are stored in post-order — every child's
index is lower than its parent's — so an evaluator can sweep them forward in
one pass.

## Predicates

Arguments name **slots** (resolved to the slot's root body), **regions**, the
robot's **sites**, or **joints**, depending on the predicate.

| Predicate | Form | True when |
|---|---|---|
| `In` | `In(obj, region)` | The object's origin is inside the region's rectangle, within the region's z half-band |
| `On` | `On(obj, region)` | Inside the rectangle and resting on it: height above the site in `[-0.005, 0.08]` m |
| `On` | `On(obj, other_obj)` | LIBERO's `check_ontop`: above the other object, **in contact** with it, xy distance under 0.03 m |
| `Near` | `Near(a, b, d)` | Origins within `d` metres |
| `Above` | `Above(a, b, margin)` | `a` is at least `margin` metres higher than `b` (no xy test) |
| `Upright` | `Upright(obj, tol)` | The body's +z axis is within `tol` of world +z, in cosine units |
| `AtRegion` | `AtRegion(site, region)` | A **site** (e.g. `robot_gripperframe`) is inside the region's volume |
| `Joint` | `Joint(joint, lt\|le\|gt\|ge, thr)` | The joint's `qpos` compares to `thr` |
| `Touching` | `Touching(a, b)` | Any body of slot `a` is in contact with any body of slot `b` |
| `And`, `Or`, `Not` | composition | As written |

`On`'s second argument decides its meaning: a region name uses the region
test; a slot name binds to the object-on-object test. A name that is both a
region and a slot is refused as ambiguous. `In` against a slot is refused —
an object has no interior; declare a `:box:` region on the container's site
instead.

**Box regions** (`region=<name>:box:<site>:...`) change what `In` and `On`
mean for that region: they evaluate LIBERO's `SiteObject.in_box` and `under`
tests in the site's own rotated frame, and an optional contact slot makes `On`
also require contact with that fixture. See [placement](/tasks/placement/)
for the region syntax.

:::note[Two ops that parse but do not run]
**`Open(joint, frac)`** parses and is refused at bind: use
`Joint(<joint>, lt|le|gt|ge, <threshold>)`, which is how LIBERO's
`Open` / `Close` / `Turnon` / `Turnoff` are translated. **`Grasped(obj)`**
parses and binds but has no defined semantics yet (see below).
:::

### Margins are part of the task

Goals are evaluated on body origins, so a margin of zero is often already
true. `Above(brick, table)` with no margin holds at reset — a brick resting on
the table is above the table body's origin — so `so101_lift_brick` uses
`Above(brick, table, 0.06)`. Likewise a region's z half-band defaults to
0.12 m if the `.family` gives none; on `so101_tabletop` that made
`AtRegion(robot_gripperframe, table_top)` a box an untrained policy satisfied
on 64 of 64 episodes. The family now declares a 0.03 m band.

Before training on a new goal, check two different things: that it is false
at reset (`tests/tasks/test_task_reset_steps.mojo` asserts this for the shipped
tasks), and that a constant action does not solve it — an untrained policy
emits a near-constant action, so whatever a constant action reaches is the
floor of every training curve. `examples/tasks/task_null_action.mojo`
measures the second.

## Tier A and Tier B

| Tier | Reads | Predicates | Where it runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Per-lane `Data` the reward kernel already has: body and site poses, `qpos`, the model's site and body tables, and the lane's contact list | everything except `Grasped` | Host and device |
| **B** | Not defined yet | `Grasped` | Refused by `require_tier_a` and by the tape encoder |

Contact-reading predicates (`Touching`, object-on-object `On`, box regions
with a contact slot) are Tier A: the batched env hands each lane's contact
list to the reward kernel, and LIBERO's most common goal predicate needs it.
A Tier B goal cannot be a reward — a task using one would train against a
constant, so it is refused rather than approximated.

## The tape

The device form of a goal is the **tape**: `noeira/tasks/tape.mojo`
`encode_goal` writes a `BoundGoal` as twelve floats — four per term
(`op, a, b, param`), three terms at most — into each lane's
`META_IDX_TASK_PARAM_0..11` words of `meta`.

- Unused terms are written as `OP_NONE` (-1). `meta` is not zeroed between
  episodes, so a shorter goal must overwrite a longer one's leftovers.
- **A goal with more than three terms is refused, not truncated.** A truncated
  goal still evaluates — to something simpler than the author wrote. This is
  why one LIBERO-10 task (`put both moka pots on the stove`, three goal atoms,
  five terms after folding) has no `.task` file.
- The parser accepts up to 16 terms; only the host evaluator can run goals
  longer than three.
- The op codes are a wire format: new ops are appended, never renumbered.

The family's region table goes to `curriculum`, shared by all lanes because a
region belongs to the family: 8 words per region, 16 regions. A goal naming a
region past the table is refused by `require_gpu_regions`, because the device
would read garbage where the host reads the right region.

### Host == tape == kernel

There are three evaluators of one goal, and they share arithmetic:

| Evaluator | File | Container |
|---|---|---|
| `eval_goal` | `noeira/tasks/eval.mojo` | a `BoundGoal` on the host |
| `eval_tape` | `noeira/tasks/tape.mojo` | the twelve-float tape on the host |
| `eval_tape_gpu` | `noeira/tasks/gpu_eval.mojo` | the tape in `meta`, inside a kernel |

The predicates themselves (`pred_in_rect`, `pred_near`, `pred_above`,
`pred_upright`, `pred_joint`, `pred_ontop`, `pred_box_in`, `pred_box_under`)
are scalar functions generic over dtype, defined once in `eval.mojo`. The host
instantiates them at float64; the kernel at float32, with no float64 anywhere
(Metal has no double). Only the loop over terms is written more than once.

The gates: `tests/tasks/test_task_tape.mojo` (host goal vs host tape),
`tests/tasks/test_tape_gpu_parity.mojo` (host vs device), and at scale
`examples/tasks/task_batched_gpu.mojo`.

## The success word

The reward hook writes one more word per lane: `META_IDX_GOAL_HELD`, 1.0 if the
goal held on the last step, else 0.0. It is written by the hook that already
evaluates the goal, so there is exactly one answer.

Success is **not** read from the reward. The reward started sparse — `+1` when
the goal held — and three files came to read `reward > 0.5` as "solved"; once
shaping was added, every shaping weight had to stay small enough to keep that
test working. Giving success its own word frees the reward to take any shape.

Evaluation drivers read the success word and, on a sample of lanes, re-check it
with the host evaluator on the downloaded state:
`examples/libero/libero_eval_batched.mojo --check-lanes K` raises on any
disagreement.

## Shaping words

The SO-101 families' reward is two per-lane `tolerance` terms in `[0, 1]`:

- a **goal term** on the tape's distance — a shortfall that is zero exactly
  when the goal holds;
- a **reach term** on the gripper-to-subject distance, for goals whose first
  term names a body (`Near`, `Above`, `On`, `In`).

Their weights and margins are four more `meta` words, `META_IDX_SHAPE_W_GOAL`,
`META_IDX_SHAPE_W_REACH`, `META_IDX_GOAL_MARGIN` and `META_IDX_REACH_MARGIN`,
written by `noeira/tasks/shaping.mojo` `shaping_words`. They are per lane
because what a weight is worth depends on the task's own distance scale:
measured on `so101_tabletop` at identical weights and margins, the three
shipped tasks got a 4.7× spread in total reward and 91× in the goal term.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| All-zero words mean **no shaping** — the sparse reward | An untouched `meta` is zero-filled, so a driver that never writes them gets the safe reward |
| Negative weights are refused | A negative weight pays the policy to move away from the goal |
| A nonzero weight with a zero margin is refused | `tolerance` with margin 0 is a hard indicator — the term silently becomes sparse |

The SAC driver exposes them as `--shape-goal`, `--shape-reach`,
`--goal-margin` and `--reach-margin`, each accepting a comma list for a
multi-task run.
